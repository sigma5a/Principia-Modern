Book I · Axioms

Three laws.
One universe.

Newton replaced a tangle of special cases with three compact rules. Together they describe how motion persists, how forces change it, and why every push comes with a partner.

I
→

Inertia

An object keeps its state of rest or uniform straight-line motion unless a net external force changes it.

ΣF = 0 ⇒ v = constant
II
↗

Force changes motion

The net force equals the rate at which momentum changes. At constant mass, this becomes the familiar form below.

ΣF = dp/dt = ma
III
⇄

Reciprocal action

When body A pushes body B, body B pushes A with an equal force in the opposite direction.

F₍A→B₎ = −F₍B→A₎

A changed cosmos

From apple
to exoplanet.

The equations gained new notation, wider reach, and relativistic corrections. The central insight remains: the same physical laws connect a falling object to the motion of worlds.

1687

Principia published

One framework joins terrestrial motion with celestial mechanics.

1846

Neptune predicted

Perturbations in Uranus’s orbit point mathematicians toward an unseen planet.

1915

Gravity becomes geometry

Einstein’s general relativity extends Newton for intense gravity and high precision.

Today

Navigation among worlds

Spaceflight still begins with Newtonian mechanics, from satellites to lunar missions.
